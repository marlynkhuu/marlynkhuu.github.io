After tracking KPIs from early POCs and watching usage shift from experiments to production agents with real backlogs of prioritized use cases, I developed and presented the strategic case to leadership: we needed to move from isolated AI experiments to a scalable, reusable development ecosystem. I owned that vision and drove the operating model to support it.

Moved away from squeaky-wheel prioritization. I reoriented the roadmap from loudest stakeholders to enterprise-wide reach and low-hanging fruit — better sequencing, faster value delivery

11 — Product judgment
Platform tradeoffs & responsible AI
Key decision — HR Knowledge Agent
The situation
Executive pressure to deliver fast. There was interest in integrating employee-specific benefits data to provide personalized answers. Risk: private employee data flowing through an LLM introduced data privacy, leakage, and compliance exposure.
My decision
I recommended a phased MVP — start with general HR knowledge; defer sensitive data integration until governance, ethics, security, and privacy reviews were complete. Required guardrails, mitigation plans, and approval dependencies before proceeding.
"I prioritized delivering value quickly, but not at the expense of introducing unmanaged risk into the organization."

12 — AI governance
AI Governance Agent
Scaling responsible AI oversight across the enterprise
The challenge

As custom GPT usage expanded enterprise-wide, the organization needed a scalable way to evaluate prompt quality, detect risk, and escalate high-risk use cases before they created exposure. As custom GPTs retired, I led the migration of this governance capability to evaluate OpenAI skills and plugins — extending coverage to the new enterprise tooling ecosystem.

The solution

I led the product definition and delivery of an agent built for Privacy and Risk teams — using five specialized judges to evaluate enterprise-created AI tools and escalate risks for review. Originally targeting custom GPTs, I adapted the product roadmap as the platform retired and migrated evaluation coverage to OpenAI skills and plugins.

13 — Leadership philosophy
How I lead
◈
Lead with business impact, not features. Every decision connects to measurable value — time saved, risk reduced, revenue enabled, or cost avoided.
◈
Translate ambiguity into structured strategy. Turn unclear opportunities and competing priorities into clear roadmaps and prioritization logic backed by data.
◈
Build scalable platforms, not one-off tools. Design reusable frameworks — playbooks, IaC templates, governance models — that make the next product faster and cheaper.
◈
Treat adoption as a first-class outcome. Deployment is not done until users are using it. I measure behavior change, not just launch dates.
◈
Influence through relationships and transparency. Trust is built through honest tradeoff conversations and candor about what's not working.
◈
Protect users and the business through responsible AI. Speed matters — but not at the cost of unmanaged risk, privacy gaps, or governance shortcuts.

"My product leadership approach is centered on turning complex operational and AI opportunities into scalable, measurable products that create business impact, reduce risk, and improve how people work."
